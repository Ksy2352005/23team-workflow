# The Restaurant Nobody Entered — Treatment

23 Team · Instagram Reel · vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) · about 59 s · Hungarian voiceover (supplied, unchanged)

The look comes from 23 Team's own character and Budapest world sheets (`refs/`), not from a library style. The full scene-by-scene plan, with copy-ready prompts, is in `PRODUCTION-PLAN.md`.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Linear day-by-day (chosen).** Morning prep → empty day 1 → empty day 2 → the first guest → the chain of guests → full room. It follows the voiceover sentence by sentence, so picture and words never fight. It also lets the empty room and the full room be shot from the **same camera position** (Scenes 3 and 10), so the change reads in a split second.
2. **Outside looking in.** The whole film stays on the street, watching the window fill up from the passers-by's point of view. It makes a strong social-proof image, but we would lose the owner's face, which the emotional turn needs.
3. **Ending first.** Open on the busy room, then rewind to the empty days. It hooks hard, but it gives the twist away, and the voiceover is written in chronological order.

## Logline and arc

A small Budapest restaurant owner opens his doors to an empty room, until one guest sits by the window and that single guest draws everyone else in.

- **Setup:** hope and care (Scenes 1–2).
- **Low point:** two empty days and doubt. This contains the film's first silence (Scenes 3–4).
- **Turn:** the doorbell rings for the first time (Scene 5).
- **Build:** the window becomes an advert, and guests arrive one by one (Scenes 6–9).
- **Ending:** a full room, then a pull back to the street, where a new passer-by stops at the glowing window. This mirrors the opening (Scenes 10–11).

## Benchmarks

- **Pixar shorts** (for example *For the Birds*, *Lava*).
  - Learn: simple, readable silhouettes; emotion through posture and timing; one clear action per shot.
  - Don't take: their characters, designs or shots.
- **Christmas TV adverts with a narrated emotional build** (for example the John Lewis spots).
  - Learn: music that holds back, then lifts on the turn; the hold on the final image.
  - Don't take: their melodies or story beats.

## Directing rules for this film

- **The owner is the only blue character inside the restaurant.** All customers are white, yellow or soft light grey, so the owner is always readable at a glance.
- **The window table is the story device.** The first guest sits there on purpose so passers-by can see them. Keep the same table in Scenes 6, 7, 9 and 10.
- **The brass doorbell stays silent until Scene 5.** Its first ring is the most important sound in the film. It follows a full silence and later rings once for each new guest.
- **Two silences:**
  - end of Scene 4, before the door opens;
  - start of Scene 11, on "Hanem arra", before the final line blooms.
- **Signature shot:** Scene 11 pulls back from the owner's face, through the window, to the street. It ends on the opening view, now full.
- **No text in any frame:** no signs, menus, clocks or logos. An optional 23 Team end card can be added in the edit.

## Score

- **Instruments:** cimbalom (a Hungarian touch), nylon-string guitar, soft pizzicato strings, brushed snare from Scene 9. This avoids the generic "piano and strings" sound.
- **Shape:** warm and hopeful (Scenes 1–2) → thins to one note (Scenes 3–4) → silence → one cimbalom note answering the bell (Scene 5) → grows with every new guest (Scenes 6–10) → drops to a held note → resolves on "bizalmat szavazott neked" (Scene 11).
